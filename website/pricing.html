~/how-the-relay-works

The relay can't read your terminal

The same paranoia that made the open-source version LAN-only, applied to going further.

01

Encrypt at your desk

Your desktop Ternimal encrypts the session stream with a per-launch key before anything leaves the machine.

02

Relay forwards ciphertext

The relay routes encrypted packets between your desktop and your devices. It holds no keys and no token — nothing to leak, nothing to subpoena.

03

Decrypt on your device

Your phone or browser decrypts locally. Sessions, scrollback and auth still live only on your hardware.

data residency: the relay stores no session content, no scrollback, no credentials — routing metadata only, discarded after connection teardown.

~/faq

Asked, answered

Can the relay see my terminal?

No. The stream is encrypted end-to-end with a per-launch key; the relay routes ciphertext only. Your token never leaves your devices either.

Do I still run the desktop app myself?

Yes — that's the point. Sessions, scrollback and auth stay on your hardware. The relay is just the road back to them.

What happens to my sessions if the relay is down, or I stop paying?

Nothing. Sessions live in your desktop Ternimal and keep running; on LAN you're never interrupted. Reconnect later and the replay catches you up.

How do I cancel?

Any time. When the subscription ends you keep the full open-source version — same binary, same features on your LAN.
